The equations below describe conventional SOC-enabled SCF. The animation uses a small, explicitly defined response model to make mixing and residuals visible. It does not calculate electronic orbitals or simulate a material.

1. The input density defines the electronic problem
\[D_k=(n_k,\mathbf m_k)\quad\longrightarrow\quad\hat H[D_k]\]

The charge and spin densities, together with the fixed crystal and chosen approximations, determine the effective Hamiltonian. It includes kinetic energy, local potentials and nonlocal pseudopotential terms carrying SOC. Four local potential fields alone do not specify it.

2. Solve for electronic states in that potential
\[\hat H[D_k]\psi_{j\mathbf q}=\epsilon_{j\mathbf q}\psi_{j\mathbf q},\qquad \psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_\uparrow\\\psi_\downarrow\end{pmatrix}\]

For the norm-conserving formulation used here, the inner eigenvalue problem yields energies and two-component spinors at reciprocal-space points q. Each spinor has two coupled complex amplitudes; these are not two independent collinear calculations. This inner solve has its own accuracy requirements.

3. Reconstruct the charge and spin density
\[n_{\rm out}(\mathbf r)=\sum_{j\mathbf q}w_{\mathbf q}f_{j\mathbf q}\,\psi^\dagger\psi\]
\[\mathbf m_{\rm out}(\mathbf r)=\sum_{j\mathbf q}w_{\mathbf q}f_{j\mathbf q}\,\psi^\dagger\boldsymbol\sigma\psi\]

Sum occupied-state contributions using occupations f and reciprocal-space weights w. The Pauli matrices σ extract the three spin components. Here m is spin density in electron-density units; conversion to a magnetic moment uses the recorded convention.

4. Measure the mismatch and construct the next input
\[R_k=D_{\mathrm{out},k}-D_k\]
\[D_{k+1}=D_k+\alpha R_k\]

The second equation is simple linear mixing. A smaller α takes a smaller step toward the output. Real QE calculations can use preconditioning and history-based mixing; this example uses linear mixing so every update can be followed directly. A smaller step is not automatically a more accurate final solution.

The exact model behind this animation

Two coefficients control spatial variations in charge (c) and z-spin density (s). Their target is (0.25, 0.60), and their initial values are (0.75, −0.80). The response is deliberately simple:

\[c_{\rm out}=0.25-1.4(c-0.25),\qquad s_{\rm out}=0.60+0.65(s-0.60)\]

Each coefficient is mixed using the selected α. The resulting errors multiply by 1−2.4α for charge and 1−0.35α for spin on each step. Negative factors reverse the sign of the error; factors near ±1 decay slowly. All three offered settings converge in this model.

\[n(x,y)=1+0.20c[\cos(2\pi x)+\cos(2\pi y)]\]
\[m_z(x,y)=0.15+0.24s\cos(2\pi x)\sin(2\pi y)\]

The cell-mean charge stays one. These fields keep n positive and |m_z|≤n throughout the offered runs; m_x=m_y=0 in this simplified illustration. The chart clips values below 1e−8 at its lower edge; the numeric readouts continue below that floor. Residuals are field RMS differences in arbitrary units, not QE’s energy-error estimate or our dataset’s L1 qualification metric. This is a fixed-point analogy, not a reduced SOC solver.
